## Direction and evidence

Prioritize Evan's approximately 30-minute budget. Propose repeating full-body A/B visits, usually three nonconsecutive days weekly: one priority lift performed separately, then two superset blocks. Under the model below, ordinary sessions contain **about ten working sets**, initially across five movements; eleven fits only if measured timing permits. Six exercises and 18–24 sets remain an extended-session option, not a requirement or a 30-minute promise.

- **Anatoly:** the attributed public account emphasizes squat/bench/deadlift, planned progression and consistent technique, describes 40–90-minute sessions, and distinguishes its sample program from his personal schedule. Borrow that emphasis, not the sample doubles or total workload. [Public account](https://anatolyfit.com/blog/how-does-anatoly-train-vladimir-shmondenko-s-real-training-routine).
- **BAU5:** borrow the guided sequence and confirmed actual reps documented in Evan's screenshots. Its numerical algorithm remains unknown. [Evidence record](GYM-EXPERIENCE.md#evidence-and-its-limits).
- The ACSM overview supports individualization and varied effective resistance programs; heavier loads favor maximal strength, while failure is not required. It does not validate our exact dose, effort targets or algorithm. [2026 overview](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/), Abstract and Discussion.
- Supersets shortened sessions with similar average adaptations in studied protocols, but raised perceived exertion; same-muscle pairings reduced volume load. These findings do not validate every pair below. [2025 meta-analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-025-02176-8), Abstract, Results.

**Every numerical default below is our reviewable heuristic, not a source-derived prescription for Evan.** Small initial volume plus low effort is a familiarization phase, not a claim of optimal long-term training.

## Preparation and first use

**Evan's starting context:** use the first visits to rehearse movements and establish current capacity. Prior lifting experience supplies context, not starting weights or a presumed ability to tolerate the former workload. Select each initial variant and load from current rehearsal/calibration; even the barbell itself is not an assumed starting load. The ordinary ten-set template is a later session shape once its movements are established, not a first-visit quota.

Use controlled reps through an established comfortable range. Stop when technique deteriorates, pain appears or continuation feels unsafe. Early practice leaves several reps available. After two completed comparable exposures without difficulty flags, the prescription exits the app's initial cautious state; this is an evidence-state transition, not verified physical readiness. Later cues can explain approximately 2–3 reps in reserve, without mandatory scoring or pretending completion measures spare capacity.

Rehearse each movement and ramp toward its planned load. Warm-ups are separate from working evidence. Extra preparation reduces optional work rather than being rushed. Starting-load selection must use current ability and available equipment, not bodyweight multipliers or old maximums; the exact exercise-specific selector remains a build blocker.

First-use limits: **two new movements per visit, three trial sets per movement, six minutes of calibration per visit**, whichever comes first. Preparation/rest remains required; reaching a cap defers work. For a weighted movement, trial reps start at its range floor. Optional **Easy / Manageable / Too hard** feedback directs the next bounded trial; skipped feedback retains uncertainty. An easy trial may move to the next permitted load, rather than spending weeks adding feather-light reps. Trial increments need the approved exercise-specific policy described below. A missed floor or difficulty report stops upward trials.

A completed floor-target trial without a reported difficulty can supply the provisional load and range-floor working target even when feedback is skipped; this infers neither Easy nor safety. With sufficient time, start separately labelled working sets; otherwise defer them. A trial's role remains calibration forever, although it can select the prescription. Before a baseline exists, unsuccessful work returns to the last manageable trial prescription or defers the movement if none exists. Two comparable completed working exposures establish a baseline. An optional “Easy” report offers faster bounded reassessment, including on the next visit when today's calibration cap is exhausted. For an established movement, permit at most one upward recalibration load step per visit, also subject to an exercise-specific cumulative increase cap that must be defined before enabling this path. Repeated Easy reports cannot chain upward steps.

**First-week example:** visit 1, A, calibrates squat and flat press; visit 2, B, calibrates hinge and pulldown; visit 3, A, calibrates row and calf raise while retaining established provisional squat/press work as time allows. Later B calibrates incline press and split squat; dead bug follows, or its matching home check supplies the same identity. Every ended visit containing a confirmed calibration or working set advances A/B, even when other movements were deferred. Thus partial onboarding cannot lock the program on A. The first visits may have fewer movements; calibration-only work earns attendance credit.

## Evidence and progression rules

Store issued prescriptions, actuals, load units/mode, variant, set role, planned sets and context. Dumbbells record weight per hand, bars total loaded weight, and machine loads their specific stack identity. Maintain an established baseline, a provisional prescription, comparable-success count, difficulty count and failures of the same proposed increase.

**Comparable starting context:** same variation, setup, load, target and planned set count. Paired versus straight sets and longer rest can still count when prescribed preparation/recovery is met and no unusual fatigue is flagged. Shorter recovery, material pre-fatiguing order changes, calibration and tagged interruption/discomfort are excluded. Excluded exposures skip counters; a return check resets them. An eligible success resets the difficulty count; an eligible short exposure resets the success count. An effort-shortened exposure counts once as difficulty even if remaining sets are stopped; untouched sets add no misses.

1. **Ordinary success:** every required set and side must independently reach target. Two comparable successes propose +1 rep per set within range, resetting success counting. This is a trial, not inferred ease.
2. **Extras with a function:** on established stable movements, the last set may add up to two controlled reps, capped by the range ceiling. If every set individually met target and the last confirmed one or two extras, one exposure can earn the same +1-rep proposal. Extras are never pooled across sets and never enlarge a load jump. Squat, hinge, new variants and return checks have no extra-rep prompt.
3. **At the ceiling:** after all sets reach their range ceiling, two comparable successes propose the smallest available external-load increase up to 5%, with targets reset to the floor. These are provisional loads. Two successes establish the new prescription and can also earn the next bounded proposal. A “Too hard” report vetoes increasing despite completed reps; absent feedback is unknown.
4. **Coarse equipment steps:** 5% limits automatic increases, not usable equipment. If the smallest step exceeds it, offer bounded calibration of that next load in the **same exercise**, within the calibration/time limits and without extras. Eligibility, maximum step, trial reps and stopping criteria require an exercise-specific policy before implementation. A permitted successful trial supplies a provisional prescription; otherwise retain the established one and revise the bridge. The screen explains this path instead of forcing an exercise swap or silently holding forever. Until that policy is defined, the feature is not build-ready. Equipment increments are an app setup input when Evan has a gym, not information he must supply now.
5. **Bodyweight/first load:** use the same rep-success gates within each variation. At its ceiling, offer a suitable harder variant or first external load through bounded first-use calibration; choose a new target. Apply no percentage to zero. If no eligible progression is available, explain the limitation and offer continued practice rather than unlimited reps.
6. **Short today:** preserve actuals, offer more rest and an easier next set; ask context at most once for the event. Lower reps by one within range, or offer a lower permitted load at the floor. This gentler provisional guidance is available without answering why. After a second short set, propose ending that movement; untouched sets remain unperformed.
7. **Baseline versus trial:** an unsuccessful increased trial returns the next exposure to the last established prescription, unless current difficulty warrants an easier provisional one. Two effort-related short exposures at baseline prompt reassessment/reduction. Three comparable short exposures with unknown cause also propose a gentler prescription, without diagnosing strength loss or requiring a cause answer. At the floor, reassess a lower load/easier variant. No automatic percentage deload follows.
8. **Plateau:** two failures of the same proposed increase persist across intervening baseline returns. Failed permitted load-calibration attempts count too, except tagged interruption/discomfort. Stop automatically repeating it; reassess rest, setup and step size. A rep bridge may add one rep to one set first; a load bridge uses bounded calibration. Unknown-cause repeats also reach this branch. If a coarse-step trial fails twice at the range ceiling and no eligible bridge exists, hold the established prescription, explain why, and offer an eligible alternative or reassessment route; never silently repeat the failed trial. Successful establishment or deliberate recalibration clears the trial-failure count; merely returning to calibration does not.
9. **Manual edits:** record the actual load, including substitutions for unavailable weights. An edited load gets provisional status and separate counters; confirmation is not app approval of an arbitrary increase and does not itself schedule further sets at that load. Offer only future prescriptions eligible under the same equipment/trial policy. Preserve the older baseline until the new prescription is established.
10. **Corrections:** recompute only unstarted guidance/counters. Completed higher-load/repetition work retains its issued prescription and corrected actuals; flag changed dependencies. Its actual successful performance still supplies evidence for that prescription, even if the earlier trigger was wrong. Never erase or retroactively lower it. Prepared current work changes only after acknowledgement; duplicate taps have one effect.

## Home sessions

One reduced-coverage template: **push-up variation + air squat**, then **glute bridge + dead bug**; two rounds each, with draft ranges **6–12, 8–15, 8–15, 6–10 per side**. Split pairs when breathing, bracing or control interferes.

For first use, perform one submaximal check up to the range ceiling, stopping with controlled reps still available. Set the provisional working target to two fewer than confirmed check reps, bounded by the range floor/ceiling. If the check ends below the floor, try an eligible easier variant or defer; never prescribe unachieved reps. The check keeps its calibration role and counts toward attendance. Floor-only home first use permits up to four check sets in total, initially one per movement, under the shared six-minute cap and required recovery. Easier/harder-variant retries consume that same check/time budget; defer as needed. Gym and new weighted-movement limits remain unchanged. An “Easy” result at the ceiling can propose a harder eligible variant next visit through calibration. The ordinary and extra-rep gates apply; a home week need not creep from six reps when a check supports more.

Floor-only options include knee push-ups; wall/incline work requires confirming a suitable surface. Split squat and single-leg bridge are separately checked candidates after their simpler movements, not automatic difficulty conversions. At a ceiling without a suitable next option, show the limit. No jumps, unstable furniture or improvised pulling anchors are assumed.

This template has **no adequately loaded pulling movement**. Identify that gap; it is a temporary alternative, not equivalent gym coverage. A four-week study in 23 young men supports progressive push-ups for upper-body strength, not our whole template or equivalence for Evan. Use one history for genuinely identical variants/setups across settings. After 14 days without that movement, use the return check below; after 42 days, bounded first-use reassessment applies. Gym attendance alone cannot refresh an unused home variant. Home work advances its own sequence and leaves the next gym template due; no catch-up backlog or equivalent-pound tonnage is created.

## Visit identity, coverage and return

Keep an unfinished visit resumable for six hours after its last activity, including across midnight. A pause over 30 minutes requires renewed preparation; resumed work carries that context. After six hours, propose closing partial and starting a distinct visit. One attendance credit per **local visit-start date**, earned by any confirmed non-warm-up set, prevents morning/evening double credit; a midnight continuation retains its original date. Calibration/partial credit stays visibly distinct from full-template completion.

End any visit with confirmed calibration/working activity by advancing the relevant rotation; otherwise keep it due. Preserve incomplete coverage. After two consecutive planned omissions of a movement pattern, put it earlier in the next applicable template or propose a shorter plan retaining it; keep demanding lifts protected. This rule prevents endless A repeats and silent disappearance of secondary work.

A 48-hour recovery interval is a soft suggestion after a substantive session, not a lockout. Preview the next workout and allow a recovery/shorter option. A brief partial visit does not automatically defer the next normal visit; consider the actual movements and sets performed.

After **14 days** without a known exercise, prepare it and use its first planned set as a labelled return check, without extras; it remains working data, not a new-exercise calibration slot. The app proposes a conservative attainable load using the exercise-specific return policy, retaining historical load as context, not readiness. Passing begins provisional re-establishment; difficulty eases remaining work. At **42 days**, use bounded first-use reassessment, spreading movements across alternating visits. Exact return-load proposals remain a build blocker. These clocks apply to exercise exposure, not attendance or external tonnage.
